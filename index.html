6 Build the test battery — AI helps (no scales needed yet)

  
A concept test asks a few standard questions. You haven't studied scales yet, so here they are in plain words — the same for any concept. The prompt turns them into a ready survey for your concept.

  

  
▸ see the battery written out for an example
    

      Example — concept: “NightTrain: spontaneous weekend rail trips for students, booked in 3 taps.”

      • Purchase intent: “How likely would you be to book a NightTrain trip in the next 3 months?” — 1 Definitely not · 2 · 3 · 4 Maybe · 5 · 6 · 7 Definitely yes.

      • Relevance: “This is made for someone like me.” — 1 Strongly disagree … 7 Strongly agree.

      • Uniqueness: “This is different from how I travel now.” — 1 … 7.

      • Believability: “I believe it can really get me a cheap trip in 3 taps.” — 1 … 7.

      • Value: “At about €39 a weekend, this is worth it.” — 1 … 7.

      • Open: “What do you like most about it?” · “What would stop you from using it?”

      Same six items work for any concept — just swap the product words. The 1–7 is a rating scale; you'll study why in Tema 5.
    

  

  
  






  
7 The protocol — choose the test

  
The protocol is HOW you run the test. First decide who sees what; the prompt then writes the full protocol (audience, people per concept, price shown or hidden, question order, rotation).

  

    
Monadic — each person sees ONE concept and rates it. Cleaner, more realistic; needs more people. Compare the three by their separate scores.

    
Comparative — each person sees all three and ranks them. Faster and fewer people; shows relative preference. Rotate the order so concept A isn't always first.

    
Protocol basics either way: one concept per screen · decide price shown or hidden (say which) · ask the SAME battery every time · define the target audience · aim for a realistic number per concept.
